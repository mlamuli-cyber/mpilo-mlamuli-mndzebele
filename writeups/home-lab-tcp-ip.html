Building a Home Lab to Practice TCP/IP & Routing

5 min read

One of the things that stuck with me from the Networking Essentials material was how different subnetting looks on paper versus when you're staring at a device that genuinely won't talk to another one. So I put together a small home lab to get past the theory and into the troubleshooting.

The setup

A basic topology: two or three routed segments, a mix of static and DHCP-assigned addressing, and a router in the middle handling inter-VLAN routing. Nothing exotic — the goal was to reproduce the kind of environment described in the coursework and actually break it on purpose.

What I practiced

  • Subnetting a /24 into smaller blocks for each segment
  • Configuring static routes between segments
  • Setting up DHCP scopes and watching lease behavior
  • Using ping, traceroute, and interface statistics to diagnose connectivity issues

Takeaway: the fastest way to actually understand the OSI model isn't memorizing the layers — it's watching where a broken configuration causes a ping to fail, and working out which layer that failure lives in.

What's next

Next step is layering basic network security on top of this lab — ACLs, port security, and testing how the setup holds up against the kind of scans covered in Cybersecurity Essentials.
